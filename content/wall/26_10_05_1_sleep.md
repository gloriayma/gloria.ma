The thing I appreciate about research fellowships is the freedom to decide what you work on, and the freedom to decide how your day goes. 

Since 9th grade (Sidhant can vouch for this one) I've had it in my head that I want to sleep at sunset and wake at sunrise. For an infinite number of reasons I've never been able to do this; right now it feels like the amount of freedom I have is also infinite, so I'm hopeful.

Today I woke up sometime around 7:30a, to sunlight (this must be the first night in years that I've slept without my eye mask.) Today was particular in that 1) I allowed myself to get out of bed with minimal struggle (almost every morning I try to argue myself back into sleep due to the knowledge that I've not been asleep long in wall-clock time) and 2) after I got up, I decided not to check the time. 

I *love* the feeling of not knowing what time it is, with assurance that this is ok. I learned the word "polychronic" from Alan a couple of weeks back, when we were talking about his experience living in Morocco. I like the idea, particularly, of letting things happen when they want to happen. When I plan too far in advance an arrangement with a friend, it feels to me as though there is a third person in the group whose wants we are trying to accommodate. As the day comes, even if neither of us are feeling up to the original time we set, we withhold these thoughts, as if to spare the feelings of our *calendars*. [Here](https://feedbackdef.substack.com/p/i-never-knew-there-are-two-and-possibly) is something I skimmed about cultures with a monochronic / polychronic relationship with time. I find it curious that if one searches up the definition of "polychronicity" Google points you to "multitasking". I have negative associations with that word, but I blame my monochronic environment for all of it - when I think about my own life, I can certainly point to how I've, at some level of organization, never successfully *not* multitasked.

In summary, I like the freedom that I've found in my research fellowships. I've been daydreaming about PhD life, but the one thing about that is I'm unsure of how much freedom I can have in choosing what I work on, if I'm anchored to one lab for years. So my current resolution is that, I'll see about it when the time comes... but while I am in my undergraduate years, I'm going to remember the freedom I have right now to work on all the things that I want to work on.
